HyperComply and Conveyor alternatives for early SaaS

If the job is filling customer security portals or running a trust center, HyperComply and Conveyor are the products to evaluate. If the job is a self-serve answer bank at draft prices of $49–$79 a month, that is a narrower tool—and QuestVault is not live SaaS yet.

Two different jobs

Search results lump every “security questionnaire tool” together. Buyers then compare a spreadsheet answer bank to a customer-trust platform and call the cheaper one incomplete. The jobs are different.

Customer-trust / questionnaire automation platforms (Conveyor, HyperComply, and peers) are built to ingest knowledge, draft answers, often share a trust center or trust page, and in Conveyor’s case automate work inside customer portals via a browser extension listed on their public pricing page. HyperComply’s AWS Marketplace listing describes Excel/Word self-import with AI autofill, plus a Full-Service line where the vendor imports and reviews under a 1–5 business day SLA, and a Trust Page sold as a separate contract unit.

A self-serve answer bank stores your reviewed Q&A, suggests fills for the next XLSX/CSV, and exports. That is the QuestVault draft: early access, not a live multi-module trust platform, not a managed services desk.

Use this page to avoid a category error—not to pretend a waitlist product replaces portal automation.

Where an answer bank loses (including QuestVault)

Be direct. A $49–$79/month library will lose these evaluations, and it should:

  • Portal automation. If security questionnaires live in OneTrust-style, ServiceNow-style, or other vendor-risk web apps, you need in-page filling or a service that operates those UIs. Conveyor’s pricing page lists a browser extension for customer portals on questionnaire automation. QuestVault’s stated workflow is file upload and XLSX export. That is a loss on purpose.
  • Trust center as the primary motion. Conveyor’s Free tier is a limited trust center (and explicitly excludes questionnaire automation). HyperComply sells Trust Page as its own AWS dimension. If your sales motion is “stop sending spreadsheets, share a gated evidence room,” buy that category.
  • Managed human review under SLA. HyperComply Full-Service on AWS starts at $10,000 per 12 months for <50 FTE. You are paying for import, completeness review, and turnaround. An early-access self-serve bank does not offer that.
  • Usage-priced enterprise packaging. Conveyor Business starts at $9,600/year with included questionnaire credits and unlimited seats. If you need Salesforce packages, SSO/SCIM, custom domains, and RFP modules, you are not in answer-bank scope.
  • Live, generally available SaaS. QuestVault is a draft / early-access waitlist product. It is not fully live SaaS. Do not budget it as a production control you can buy today.

Losing those deals is not a messaging failure. It is the product boundary.

Where a $49–$79 self-serve answer bank wins

The win condition is early B2B SaaS with recurring files, not a trust-center program:

  • Inbound work is XLSX or CSV more often than a portal.
  • A founder, CS lead, or security owner already answers from memory and old workbooks.
  • You will not operate a knowledge graph, credit packs, and a public trust site this quarter.
  • You want suggested fills from your past Q&A, then a human review step, then export.
  • Annual questionnaire-platform list prices ($6k Respond AI floor on HyperComply’s AWS table; $9,600 Conveyor Business starting price) are the wrong first check for a team that does a handful of questionnaires per quarter.

Draft QuestVault prices on the waitlist page are $49/month Starter and $79/month Team, with a 14-day trial in the current draft. Those numbers may change before launch. They exist to price an answer library, not to undercut a portal product on features the library does not have.

If that is your stage, start with the pillar workflow and an answer-bank structure. Add SIG/CAIQ discipline when those files appear: spreadsheet filler for startups.

How to choose without a bake-off theatre

  1. Count artifacts. Last two quarters: how many portals vs how many spreadsheets? If portals dominate, shortlist portal tools first.
  2. Count operators. One owner with a library is an answer-bank problem. A security team plus sales plus a customer-facing evidence site is a trust-platform problem.
  3. Price the real SKU. Conveyor Free does not include questionnaire automation. HyperComply Trust Page is not inside the Respond AI line item. Compare the SKU you would actually run.
  4. Keep review. AI autofill is a first pass on both enterprise listings. Your team still owns the send. That is true of QuestVault’s draft as well: it does not complete questionnaires automatically.
  5. Do not buy vapor as if it were GA. QuestVault is early access. If you need production software this month, evaluate generally available vendors and a DIY sheet. Join the waitlist only if the narrower job is what you want next.

The honest summary: lose to portal automation; win on a $49–$79 self-serve answer bank—and only when that bank exists as a product you can use. Right now it is a waitlist.
