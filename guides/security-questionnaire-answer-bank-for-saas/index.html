A security questionnaire answer bank for SaaS teams

An answer bank is not a dump of old Excel files. It is one reviewed library of control statements you can map onto the next buyer spreadsheet without rewriting the security story from scratch.

What an answer bank is

A security questionnaire answer bank is a governed set of your answers—owned, dated, and cited—used as the source of truth when a customer sends a new VSA, SIG-style workbook, CAIQ spreadsheet, or portal export.

It is not:

  • A folder named “security questionnaires 2024–2026.”
  • A chatbot that invents controls you did not implement.
  • A GRC platform, a policy management suite, or a claim that you hold a particular audit report. If you have a SOC 2 report, cite the relevant section as evidence. If you do not, do not write as if you do.
  • A substitute for legal or security review on novel, sensitive, or out-of-date rows.

The commercial reason to build one is simple: inbound questions overlap. Encryption, identity, logging, incident response, subprocessors, and backup recur. The wording changes. Your control should not.

Columns that make answers reusable

Copy a “yes” without context and it expires. Keep the fields below and the same sentence can survive the next file.

Field Why it exists
Topic / domain Grouping for search: access, encryption, privacy, resilience, vendors. Use your taxonomy; you do not need to reproduce a licensed questionnaire’s domains.
Canonical answer The approved wording. Written for reuse, then lightly edited to match the buyer’s prompt.
Evidence / citation Where a reviewer can check the claim: policy title and section, architecture note, ticket, or report section. A URL or filename beats “see Slack.”
Owner The person who can defend the row. On an early team this is often a founder, CS lead, or the engineer who runs production access.
Reviewer / status Draft, needs review, reviewed, exception. Status is how you stop shipping a half-edited cell.
Last verified The date someone confirmed the control still matches reality. Stale dates are a queue, not a decoration.
Scope notes Product, environment, region, or shared-responsibility boundaries. SaaS answers go wrong when they describe the customer’s cloud as if it were yours, or vice versa.
Exception / compensating control Honest gaps with a dated plan. Inventing a control to close a deal is worse than a clear “not yet.”
Customer-specific overlay Stored off the canonical row so one logo’s contractual language does not leak into the next workbook.

Do not paste licensed questionnaires into a public template. SIG content is licensed by Shared Assessments. CAIQ is published by the Cloud Security Alliance for use as their spreadsheet. Your bank holds answers and evidence. The inbound file from the buyer holds their questions.

How SaaS teams actually use it

Early B2B SaaS usually has three people in the loop: whoever owns the customer relationship, whoever can speak to production, and whoever will be blamed if a sentence is wrong. The bank has to survive that split.

  1. Ingest past Q&A. XLSX and CSV are the native formats. Pull pairs from completed customer files. Tag the source file so you can see which deal produced the wording.
  2. Deduplicate. Five answers to “MFA for administrative access” means you do not have a bank; you have an argument. Pick one canonical row and record exceptions separately.
  3. Match the new file. When the next spreadsheet arrives, map each inbound question to the closest library row. Close matches get a suggested fill. Weak matches stay blank.
  4. Edit for the prompt. Buyers use drop-downs, character limits, and “describe / attach evidence.” Your canonical paragraph may need to shrink, split, or point to an attachment. That is editing, not authorship from zero.
  5. Export in their shape. Return XLSX (or the portal’s export) with their IDs intact. Internal pretty-print is optional. Their template is not.

Review without a GRC team

You do not need a six-person GRC org to keep a bank honest. You need rules that fit a small team:

  • No send from “draft.” Suggested fills start as draft. Export is for reviewed rows plus explicitly accepted edits.
  • Material change triggers. New subprocessor, new region, new identity provider, or a production incident: re-verify the affected topics the same week.
  • Blank is allowed. A blank cell with an owner follow-up is cleaner than a confident sentence you cannot cite.
  • The bank does not certify you. Completing questionnaires is not the same as holding an audit opinion. Do not imply certifications you have not been issued.

When a sheet is enough—and when it is not

Stay in Sheets or Excel while one person can still find the row, the date is current, and inbound volume is occasional. Move to a dedicated answer-bank tool when matching and review status are the bottleneck, or when CS and security are editing two copies of the truth.

Do not jump to an enterprise questionnaire suite just because the library is messy.
